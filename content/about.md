<h2 id="purpose">Our purpose</h2>

**ASCEND stands for Aerial Science, Collaboration, and Education Network for Discovery.** The project connects student teams at two-year community colleges and four-year universities with faculty mentors, university pod leads, and subject matter experts.

Our shared focus is the movement of wildfire smoke and other pollutants through the atmosphere. Teams will use balloons, unmanned aircraft systems (UAS), and surface observations to investigate conditions from near the ground to the stratosphere, then connect those measurements with complementary NASA datasets.

ASCEND brings education and research together. Students participate in the full process: defining a question, developing instruments and platforms, collecting measurements, evaluating data, and communicating what they find. The project emphasizes access for community colleges and institutions with limited research infrastructure.

<h2 id="goals">Five project goals</h2>

### 1. Build a sustainable national research network

Connect undergraduate teams through shared atmospheric science and engineering work. Students will learn to operate reusable measurement platforms and collect, clean, analyze, map, and share data across institutions.

### 2. Improve understanding of air pollution transport

Bring together observations from different platforms, locations, and heights. Teams will interpret their measurements alongside NASA data, collaborate with the broader scientific community, and learn from experts in academia, government, and industry.

### 3. Expand institutional research capacity

Help more colleges and universities engage students in Earth system research using remote sensing, vertical profiles, and geospatial analysis. Shared tools and cross-institutional support are intended to make participation practical for teams with different starting points.

### 4. Prepare students for STEM careers

Develop hands-on experience with atmospheric sensors, aerospace platforms, and data analysis. Scientific communication, collaborative problem solving, and project management are part of the work, supported by connections to professionals and career pathways.

### 5. Make experiential research last

Develop reusable curriculum, implementation guides, faculty training, and shared research infrastructure. The goal is to help small, emerging, and two-year institutions sustain research opportunities and partnerships beyond the project period.

<h2 id="learning">Learning through research</h2>

Teams can contribute through platform development, field measurements, data analysis, or a combination of these activities. Training is intended to meet teams at their current experience level and help them build toward more independent research.

Students will practice:

- Turning a research question into a measurement plan.
- Integrating sensors, data logging, telemetry, and aerial platforms.
- Evaluating calibration, measurement uncertainty, and data quality.
- Comparing observations across platforms, institutions, and NASA datasets.
- Mapping results and communicating findings through reports, posters, and presentations.

Explore the [seven research questions](/science/) or learn [how teams and pods work together](/project/). The Learning Library supports this work with books, presentations, build files, and resources for faculty mentors and pod leads.
