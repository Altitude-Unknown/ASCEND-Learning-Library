<h2 id="shared-question">Our shared science question</h2>

**How does the vertical structure of wildfire smoke and other pollutants influence downwind air quality, satellite observations, and pollutant transport?**

ASCEND’s research plan brings together measurements at different heights and locations to investigate that question. Student teams will help refine methods, choose instruments, and design data collection strategies with faculty mentors, pod leads, and subject matter experts.

The seven questions below guide planned investigations. They describe what teams aim to learn; they are not findings or completed results.

<h2 id="rq1">RQ1 · Vertical distribution and transport</h2>

**How is the distribution of wildfire smoke with height related to its horizontal transport and to air quality at the surface downwind?**

Teams will examine particulate measurements, including PM2.5, alongside atmospheric conditions and other measurements such as carbon dioxide where supported by their instruments. Coordinated balloon, UAS, and surface observations will help connect elevated smoke layers with conditions closer to the ground.

<h2 id="rq2">RQ2 · Interpreting satellite observations</h2>

**How can detailed balloon and UAS profiles improve the interpretation of satellite measurements, including TEMPO observations?**

Teams will compare measurements at specific heights with satellite products that describe conditions through an atmospheric column. The investigation asks how vertical profiles can help interpret complex smoke layers and differences between satellite and surface observations.

<h2 id="rq3">RQ3 · Clouds, dense smoke, and observation gaps</h2>

**What can airborne measurements add when clouds or dense smoke limit satellite and ground-based remote-sensing observations?**

Planned comparisons with satellite and AERONET observations will examine what balloon measurements reveal during gaps in remote-sensing coverage. Teams will consider the conditions, timing, and limitations of each measurement method.

<h2 id="rq4">RQ4 · Sensor performance in motion</h2>

**How do ascent rate, vibration, and airflow affect a sensor’s readings compared with stationary measurements and calibrated reference instruments?**

Teams will compare candidate particulate sensors under controlled conditions before interpreting flight data. They will investigate sensor placement, sampling flow, and platform motion, and evaluate any changes through follow-up comparisons.

<h2 id="rq5">RQ5 · From individual monitors to landscape-scale air quality</h2>

**How can vertical profiles and horizontal transects help us understand how well a surface monitoring location represents a larger area?**

UAS measurements will support comparisons of particulate concentrations across locations and heights. Teams will investigate spatial variation and how local terrain and surroundings affect the interpretation of a fixed monitoring site.

<h2 id="rq6">RQ6 · Atmospheric stability, terrain, and changing surface conditions</h2>

**How do atmospheric stability and local topography interact with short-term changes in near-surface PM2.5 and PM10?**

The research plan combines UAS and radiosonde profiles with surface and balloon observations. Measurements around morning and evening transitions will help teams examine the relationship between atmospheric structure and changing surface air quality.

<h2 id="rq7">RQ7 · Upper-atmosphere structure and long-range smoke transport</h2>

**How do the free atmosphere and the region around the tropopause influence the long-range transport of wildfire aerosols?**

Planned balloon investigations will examine smoke transport near the boundary between the troposphere and stratosphere. Measurements of atmospheric structure and, where equipped, ozone will support questions about smoke associated with fire-driven thunderstorm events and its movement over large distances.

<h2 id="platforms">Complementary measurement platforms</h2>

Each platform contributes a different part of the investigation:

- **Small balloons and radiosondes:** profiles of temperature, pressure, humidity, and wind to characterize atmospheric structure and provide reference observations.
- **High-altitude balloons:** student-integrated payloads for particulate measurements and other observations through a deeper atmospheric column.
- **Fixed-wing UAS:** measurements across horizontal paths to investigate spatial variability near the surface.
- **Multirotor UAS:** detailed vertical profiles in the lower atmosphere.
- **Surface instruments and NASA datasets:** complementary observations for comparison, interpretation, and assessment of data quality.

Shared sensing and data-logging approaches are intended to make observations easier to compare across platforms. Teams will document measurement conditions, evaluate data quality, and work with experts before drawing conclusions.
