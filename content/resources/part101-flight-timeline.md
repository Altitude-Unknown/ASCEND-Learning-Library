## About this activity

**Goal:** plan every record and call to ATC that Part 101 requires during and after a balloon flight.

Read [Balloon position reports](/part101/textbook/part-101-for-high-altitude-balloons/#position-reports) through [Derelict balloons](/part101/textbook/part-101-for-high-altitude-balloons/#derelict-balloons) first.

## Part 1: A sample flight

A covered balloon (sample data) launches at 8:00 a.m. Its tracker reports the position every minute, and the team records a position in the flight log at 8:00 a.m. The prediction says descent begins about 10:45 a.m. (the balloon bursts), and the payload lands about 11:30 a.m. Between 9:10 and 11:20 a.m. the ground station receives nothing; it hears the tracker again at 11:20 a.m. The team recovers the payload at 12:40 p.m.

<ol class="activity-questions">
<li>What call does the team make at 8:00 a.m., and to whom?</li>
<li>By what times must the team record positions during the flight, if nothing goes wrong?</li>
<li>When must the team call with the report given before descent, and what five items does it include?</li>
<li>The last position recorded before the gap is from 9:10 a.m. When must the team notify ATC about the gap, and what must the notice include?</li>
<li>What call does the team make at 11:20 a.m.?</li>
<li>When does the team give the last notice, and what does it say?</li>
<li>Why does ATC care about these calls? Use JO 7110.65BB 9-6-1 in your answer.</li>
</ol>

<details>
<summary>Check your answers</summary>
<ol>
<li>The launch notice: the launch time, immediately after launch, to the nearest FAA or military ATC facility (§ 101.37(d)).</li>
<li>At least every two hours (§ 101.39(a)(1)): by 10:00 a.m. after the 8:00 a.m. record, then by 12:00 p.m., and so on. Many teams record more often. Forward reports to ATC only if ATC requests them (§ 101.39(a)(2)).</li>
<li>One hour before descent begins: about 9:45 a.m. It includes the current geographical position, the altitude, the forecast time of penetration of 60,000 feet (if applicable), the forecast trajectory for the rest of the flight, and the forecast time and location of impact (§ 101.39(b)). In this flight the tracker is silent at 9:45 a.m., so the team has no current position: one sensible approach is to give the last recorded position (9:10 a.m.) and say that tracking has been lost.</li>
<li>When no position has been recorded for a two-hour period: by 11:10 a.m., two hours after the 9:10 a.m. record. The notice includes the last recorded position (9:10 a.m.) and any revision of the forecast trajectory (§ 101.39(c)).</li>
<li>Tracking is re-established, so notify the nearest FAA ATC facility immediately (§ 101.39(c)).</li>
<li>When the operation is ended, notify the nearest FAA ATC facility (§ 101.39(d)).</li>
<li>Controllers post the flight on flight progress strips from the prelaunch notice, follow it by radar or other means, and give traffic advisories to aircraft with the balloon’s position, direction, and altitude. Your notices and reports are part of how they keep other aircraft clear of it.</li>
</ol>
</details>
