## About this activity

**Goal:** apply the five operating limitations of 14 CFR 101.33 to a launch decision.

Read [Operating limitations](/part101/textbook/part-101-for-high-altitude-balloons/#operating-limitations) and [Reading clouds and visibility](/part101/textbook/part-101-for-high-altitude-balloons/#reading-the-weather) first. Cloud amounts follow AIM 7-1-28: FEW is more than 0/8 to 2/8, SCT 3/8 to 4/8, BKN 5/8 to 7/8, and OVC 8/8. Visibility is in statute miles (SM).

Each balloon below is covered by Part 101. The weather is for the whole planned path and time of flight unless it says otherwise. For each one, decide **go** or **no-go** and cite the paragraph of § 101.33 that decides it.

## Scenarios

<ol class="activity-questions">
<li>Launch from a school field outside any airport surface area. Forecast: visibility 10 SM, clouds FEW050.</li>
<li>Same field. Forecast: visibility 10 SM, a broken layer at 9,000 feet (BKN090).</li>
<li>Same field. Forecast: visibility 4 SM in haze, sky clear.</li>
<li>Same field. Forecast: visibility 7 SM, a scattered layer at 4,000 feet (SCT040).</li>
<li>Launch from a field inside the Class D surface area of a nearby airport. Weather is good, and the team has not contacted ATC.</li>
<li>Good weather. The launch field is beside a park where a festival with thousands of people is under way, and the wind will carry the balloon over it during the climb.</li>
<li>Good weather. The flight prediction puts the landing in the middle of a town.</li>
<li>The balloon is climbing through 30,000 feet when the team learns an overcast layer has formed above it, along its path.</li>
</ol>

<details>
<summary>Check your answers</summary>
<ol>
<li><strong>Go</strong> (on these limitations). FEW is not more than five-tenths; 10 SM is not less than 5 miles.</li>
<li><strong>No-go, § 101.33(b).</strong> BKN is 5/8 to 7/8, more than five-tenths, and the balloon will climb through 9,000 feet.</li>
<li><strong>No-go, § 101.33(c).</strong> Visibility less than 5 miles below 60,000 feet standard pressure altitude.</li>
<li><strong>Go</strong> (on these limitations). SCT is at most 4/8, exactly half, which is not more than five-tenths; 7 SM is at least 5 miles.</li>
<li><strong>No-go, § 101.33(a),</strong> unless ATC authorizes it. The balloon would be below 2,000 feet above the surface inside a Class D surface area.</li>
<li><strong>No-go, § 101.33(d).</strong> Over an open-air assembly of people not associated with the flight during the first 1,000 feet of ascent.</li>
<li><strong>No-go, § 101.33(e),</strong> if the landing could create a hazard to people or property not associated with the flight. Change the launch site or timing so the prediction lands somewhere safe.</li>
<li>The limitation applies at any altitude, so the flight can’t continue into that layer: § 101.35(a) requires the operator to activate the cut-down and termination devices when weather conditions are less than those prescribed.</li>
</ol>
</details>

## Your flight

<ol class="activity-questions" start="9">
<li>Find a forecast for your next planned launch. Write down the cloud cover and visibility for the path and time of the flight.</li>
<li>Go through § 101.33(a) to (e) one at a time. Is your flight a go? Which paragraph would most likely stop it?</li>
</ol>
