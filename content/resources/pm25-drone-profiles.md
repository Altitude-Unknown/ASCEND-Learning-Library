## About this lab

**Goal:** watch the boundary layer change through a day.

**Time:** one flying day, plus analysis.

**Materials:** a drone flown under Part 107 by a certificated remote pilot, a PM2.5 payload logging PM2.5, RH, temperature, pressure, and GPS (for example the ASCEND Logger), a second sensor or reference monitor on the ground, and the [safety checklist](/pm25/textbook/measuring-pm25-from-the-sky/#safety-checklist).

Read [UAS or HAB?](/pm25/textbook/measuring-pm25-from-the-sky/#uas-or-hab) through [Safety checklist](/pm25/textbook/measuring-pm25-from-the-sky/#safety-checklist) first, and complete the [profile prediction](/resources/pm25-profile-prediction/) for each flight.

## Plan

1. Choose a site where you may fly to 400 feet above ground level, and get any airspace authorization you need.
2. Plan three flights: around sunrise, around midday, and in the late afternoon.
3. Mount the inlet above the rotors on a mast, or on a boom beyond the rotor disk. If your team flies a fixed-wing aircraft, profile with a slow, circling climb and keep the inlet out of the propeller wash.

## Each flight

1. Run the safety checklist and log a ground reading next to the second sensor or reference monitor.
2. Hover for 1–2 minutes at 2 m for a clean surface value.
3. Climb slowly (about 1–2 m/s) to 400 feet, or pause for 10–30 s every 10–20 m.
4. Descend the same way, so you can compare the up and down legs.

## Deliverable

Three profiles of PM2.5, temperature, and RH, overlaid on shared axes, with a paragraph explaining how the boundary layer changed between flights.

<ol class="activity-questions">
<li>Did you see an inversion at sunrise? At what height?</li>
<li>How did surface PM2.5 change from sunrise to midday, and why?</li>
<li>Did the up and down legs agree? If not, what does that say about sensor lag?</li>
</ol>
