## About this activity

**Goal:** check a payload design against 14 CFR 101.35, the equipment and marking rules for a balloon covered by Part 101.

Read [Equipment and marking](/part101/textbook/part-101-for-high-altitude-balloons/#equipment-and-marking) and [Lights, antennas, and suspension lines](/part101/textbook/part-101-for-high-altitude-balloons/#lights-and-markings) first.

## Part 1: A sample design

A team plans a daytime flight. Their design has:

- one cut-down: a hot-wire cutter fired by a radio command, powered by the tracker’s battery;
- a second cut-down: a hot-wire cutter fired by a timer, powered by the same battery;
- a parachute in a bright orange color, and a 40-foot suspension line below it;
- no radar reflector;
- no trailing antenna.

<ol class="activity-questions">
<li>Do the two cut-downs meet § 101.35(a)(1)? Explain.</li>
<li>Does the design show two independent ways to terminate the flight of the balloon envelope (§ 101.35(a)(2))? What else would you need to know?</li>
<li>What is missing for § 101.35(a)(3)?</li>
<li>Does the 40-foot suspension line need bands or streamers for a daytime flight? Why?</li>
<li>Do they need lights? What would change that?</li>
</ol>

<details>
<summary>Check your answers</summary>
<ol>
<li>No. Both cutters share one battery, so one failure (a dead or disconnected battery) stops both. They do not operate independently of each other.</li>
<li>Not from this list. A payload cut-down separates the payload; § 101.35(a)(2) separately requires at least two independent methods for terminating the flight of the balloon envelope. Ask how the envelope’s flight ends, and whether two independent methods do it.</li>
<li>A radar reflective device or material on the balloon envelope that presents an echo to surface radar from 200 MHz to 2,700 MHz.</li>
<li>No. Section 101.35(d) applies to a suspension device more than 50 feet long between sunrise and sunset; 40 feet is not more than 50.</li>
<li>Not for a daytime flight. Lights are required below 60,000 feet standard pressure altitude between sunset and sunrise (§ 101.35(b)); a flight that could still be below 60,000 feet after sunset would need them.</li>
</ol>
</details>
