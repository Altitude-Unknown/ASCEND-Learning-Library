## About this activity

**Goal:** make a testable prediction of a vertical profile before you fly, then check it.

**Materials:** graph paper or a drawing app, the [University of Wyoming atmospheric soundings](https://weather.uwyo.edu/upperair/sounding.shtml) (choose the radiosonde station nearest your site; soundings are usually launched at 00 and 12 UTC), a weather forecast, and the [AirNow Fire and Smoke Map](https://fire.airnow.gov/).

Read [Layers you will fly through](/pm25/textbook/measuring-pm25-from-the-sky/#layers) through [Reading a profile](/pm25/textbook/measuring-pm25-from-the-sky/#reading-a-profile) first.

## Part 1: Before the flight

<ol class="activity-questions">
<li>Write down the planned flight time, the platform, and the highest altitude it will reach.</li>
<li>Open the most recent sounding from the station nearest your site. Is there a temperature inversion near the ground? At what height does the temperature stop rising (or start falling)?</li>
<li>Will the flight be at night, near sunrise, at midday, or in the late afternoon? Based on the daily cycle, how deep do you expect the boundary layer to be?</li>
<li>Is there smoke on the Fire and Smoke Map, or a smoke forecast for your area? Could there be a lofted layer?</li>
<li>Sketch three side-by-side profiles with height on the vertical axis: PM2.5, temperature, and relative humidity. Mark where you expect the top of the boundary layer and any other layers.</li>
</ol>

## Part 2: After the flight

<ol class="activity-questions" start="6">
<li>Plot your measured profiles on the same axes as your sketch.</li>
<li>Where did you find the top of the boundary layer, and what told you (temperature, humidity, PM2.5, or all three)?</li>
<li>Where did your prediction match, and where did it miss? Give one reason for each miss.</li>
<li>Did PM2.5 change anywhere without a matching change in temperature or humidity? If so, what might explain it?</li>
</ol>
