## About this lab

**Goal:** measure PM2.5 through the whole troposphere and identify each layer.

**Time:** one launch day, plus analysis.

**Materials:** a balloon payload with PM2.5, temperature, RH, and pressure sensors logging to an SD card, insulated for the cold; redundant tracking (for example YELLOWSTONE plus a GPS tracker); a balloon, gas, and parachute; and a recovery team.

Read [Payload design](/pm25/textbook/measuring-pm25-from-the-sky/#payload-design) through [Safety checklist](/pm25/textbook/measuring-pm25-from-the-sky/#safety-checklist) first.

## Before launch

1. **Check Part 101.** Weigh and measure every payload package and the suspension line, and decide whether Part 101 Subpart D applies, using the [Part 101 classification activity](/resources/part101-classify-payloads/).
2. **Predict.** Run the [CUSF Landing Predictor](https://predict.sondehub.org/) for several launch times and sites, and choose one that lands safely, away from cities, airports, and water.
3. **Test.** Run a cold test in a freezer and, if you can, a vacuum-chamber test.
4. **Predict the profile.** Complete the [profile prediction](/resources/pm25-profile-prediction/) worksheet.

## Launch day

1. Run the safety checklist, log a ground reading next to a reference, and notify the local air traffic facility.
2. Launch, track, and record the position throughout the flight.
3. Recover the payload with the landowner’s permission.

## Deliverable

A full-atmosphere profile of PM2.5, temperature, and RH from the ascent, with the boundary layer top, any lofted layers, and the tropopause marked.

<ol class="activity-questions">
<li>At what height was the top of the boundary layer, and what showed it?</li>
<li>Did you find any lofted layers? Could they be smoke or dust? How would you check (satellite smoke products, HYSPLIT)?</li>
<li>Where was the tropopause, and how did you find it?</li>
<li>Which parts of the profile do you trust least, and why?</li>
</ol>
