## About this activity

**Goal:** prepare the prelaunch notice of 14 CFR 101.37(a), so the call to the nearest FAA ATC facility is quick and complete.

Read [The prelaunch notice](/part101/textbook/part-101-for-high-altitude-balloons/#prelaunch-notice) and [Cancellation and launch notices](/part101/textbook/part-101-for-high-altitude-balloons/#cancellation-and-launch-notices) first.

## Part 1: A sample flight

A team’s flight plan (sample data, not a real flight):

- **Balloon identification:** ASCEND Team 4, Flight 2
- **Launch:** Saturday, April 18, at 9:30 a.m. local time, from a school field (latitude 45.6612° N, longitude 111.0450° W)
- **Balloon:** latex, filled to about 7 feet in diameter and 8 feet long at launch
- **Suspension:** parachute and line, 30 feet from the balloon to the payload
- **Payload:** one package, 5 pounds 6 ounces
- **Trailing antenna:** none
- **Flight prediction:** climbs at about 1,000 feet per minute to burst at about 95,000 feet; reaches 60,000 feet about 1 hour after launch and burst about 1 hour 35 minutes after launch; drifts east-northeast; lands about 2 hours 15 minutes after launch, 70 miles east-northeast of the launch site

<ol class="activity-questions">
<li>What is the earliest and the latest time the team may give the prelaunch notice?</li>
<li>Fill in the eight items of § 101.37(a) from the plan above.</li>
<li>Item 5 asks for the estimated time to cruising altitude or 60,000 feet standard pressure altitude, “whichever is lower.” Which applies here, and what is the time?</li>
<li>The launch slips to 10:15 a.m. What must the team do?</li>
<li>The team gives the notice, then cancels because of wind. What must they do?</li>
</ol>

<details>
<summary>Check your answers</summary>
<ol>
<li>Earliest: 9:30 a.m. Friday, April 17 (24 hours before). Latest: 3:30 a.m. Saturday, April 18 (6 hours before).</li>
<li>
(1) ASCEND Team 4, Flight 2.
(2) Saturday, April 18, 9:30 a.m. local.
(3) The school field at 45.6612° N, 111.0450° W.
(4) Cruising altitude: this latex balloon climbs until it bursts rather than cruising. The rule doesn’t say what to give in that case; one reasonable answer is the expected burst altitude, about 95,000 feet, saying that the balloon climbs to burst.
(5) The forecast trajectory (east-northeast) and about 1 hour to 60,000 feet.
(6) Balloon about 8 feet long and 7 feet in diameter; suspension 30 feet; payload 5 pounds 6 ounces; no trailing antenna.
(7) About 2 hours 15 minutes.
(8) About 11:45 a.m., about 70 miles east-northeast of the launch site.
</li>
<li>60,000 feet is lower than the expected maximum altitude, so give the time to 60,000 feet: about 1 hour after launch.</li>
<li>A 45-minute slip is more than plus or minus 30 minutes, so give the ATC facility an amended launch time (§ 101.37(a)(2)).</li>
<li>Immediately notify the nearest FAA ATC facility that the operation is canceled (§ 101.37(c)).</li>
</ol>
</details>
