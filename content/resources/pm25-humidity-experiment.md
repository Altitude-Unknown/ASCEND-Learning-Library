## About this lab

**Goal:** see for yourself why humidity is the biggest error source for optical PM2.5 sensors.

**Time:** one class session.

**Materials:** one PM2.5 sensor with a relative humidity (RH) and temperature sensor logging at the same time, a large clear plastic box with a loose lid (a humid box), or a bathroom with a shower, and a way to raise humidity slowly (a pan of warm water in the box, or a running hot shower). A steady particle level helps: run the test in room air that is not changing.

Keep electronics away from dripping water and condensation. Stop the test if water forms on the sensor or board.

Read [Main error sources](/pm25/textbook/measuring-pm25-from-the-sky/#error-sources) first.

## Procedure

1. Log PM2.5, RH, and temperature together, starting in dry room air, for 10 minutes.
2. Raise the humidity slowly over 20–30 minutes, for example by adding warm water to the box or running the shower.
3. Note the time if you see fog or condensation.
4. Bring the sensor back to dry air and keep logging for 10 more minutes.

## Analysis and deliverable

Plot PM2.5 against RH (RH on the horizontal axis). Mark where the readings start to climb.

<ol class="activity-questions">
<li>At what RH did PM2.5 start to rise? How does that compare with the 70–80% range in the chapter?</li>
<li>How much higher was PM2.5 at the highest RH than in dry air, as a percent?</li>
<li>Did the readings come back down in dry air? What does that tell you about whether the particles changed or just absorbed water?</li>
<li>What RH limit would you use to flag airborne data, and why?</li>
</ol>
