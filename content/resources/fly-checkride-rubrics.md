## About these checkrides

The five airplane checkrides take a student from a first flight on a buddy box to a first solo. For each flight, the instructor scores every skill as Beginner, Intermediate, or Mastery. A student needs Mastery in all skills for a solo flight, and the instructor can still take control during the solo.

Instructors can print this page or the lab manual to record scores and signatures.

<h2 id="flight-1">1st Flight Airplane</h2>
<p><strong>Goal:</strong> Demonstrate basic in-flight control of the airplane with assistance of an instructor using a buddy-box. Perform straight and level flight, turns to the left and right. Take off and landings demonstrated by the instructor.</p>
<div class="textbook-table activity-table" role="region" aria-label="1st Flight Airplane rubric" tabindex="0"><table><caption>1st Flight Airplane rubric</caption><thead><tr><th scope="col">Demonstrated skill</th><th scope="col">Beginner</th><th scope="col">Intermediate</th><th scope="col">Mastery</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th scope="row">Maintain straight and level flight flying away from you.</th><td>Student struggles to maintain positive control, instructor has to frequently take control.</td><td>Aircraft maintains altitude to within +/- 50 feet, some instructor input required.</td><td>Aircraft maintains altitude to within +/- 50 feet, no instructor input required.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Maintain straight and level flight flying toward you.</th><td>Student struggles to maintain positive control, instructor has to frequently take control.</td><td>Aircraft maintains altitude to within +/- 50 feet, some instructor input required, and/or some evidence of disorientation.</td><td>Aircraft maintains altitude to within +/- 50 feet, no instructor input required, and no evidence of disorientation.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Perform basic turns to the left and right while maintaining positive control.</th><td>Some attempt at turns are made but student gets disoriented, losses control, or aircraft ends up out of operational safety limits.</td><td>Turns to the left and right are made with moderate changes in altitude (+/- 50 feet) and pitch. Turns may be uncoordinated.</td><td>Coordinated turns to the left and right are made with little to no changes in altitude (+/- 20 feet).</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<h3>Skills introduced and demonstrated by the instructor</h3><p class="small">May be performed in front of a large group one time.</p><ul class="skills-checklist">
<li>Hand Launch take-off</li>
<li>Perform slow flight without loss-of-control of the aircraft.</li>
<li>Left-hand traffic pattern</li>
<li>Landing</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="flight-2">2nd Flight Airplane</h2>
<p><strong>Goal:</strong> Demonstrate basic in-flight control of the airplane with assistance of an instructor using a buddy-box. Perform straight and level flight, turns to the left and right. Slow flight and basic throttle control will be practiced. Student should attempt to make 360 circuit around flying field without instructor taking over.</p>
<div class="textbook-table activity-table" role="region" aria-label="2nd Flight Airplane rubric" tabindex="0"><table><caption>2nd Flight Airplane rubric</caption><thead><tr><th scope="col">Demonstrated skill</th><th scope="col">Beginner</th><th scope="col">Intermediate</th><th scope="col">Mastery</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th scope="row">Maintain straight and level flight flying away from you.</th><td>Student struggles to maintain positive control, instructor has to frequently take control.</td><td>Aircraft maintains altitude to within +/- 50 feet, some instructor input required.</td><td>Aircraft maintains altitude to within +/- 50 feet, no instructor input required.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Maintain straight and level flight flying toward you.</th><td>Student struggles to maintain positive control, instructor has to frequently take control.</td><td>Aircraft maintains altitude to within +/- 50 feet, some instructor input required, and/or some evidence of disorientation.</td><td>Aircraft maintains altitude to within +/- 50 feet, no instructor input required, and no evidence of disorientation.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Perform basic turns to the left and right while maintaining positive control and maintain altitude to +/- 20 feet.</th><td>Some attempt at turns are made but student gets disoriented, losses control, or aircraft ends up out of operational safety limits.</td><td>Turns to the left and right are made with moderate changes in altitude (+/- 50 feet) and pitch. Turns may be uncoordinated.</td><td>Coordinated turns to the left and right are made with little to no changes in altitude (+/- 20 feet).</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Perform slow flight while maintaining positive control of aircraft.</th><td>Student attempts slow flight but is unable to maintain positive control for more than a few seconds before control is taken. Student often stalls the aircraft or otherwise loses control.</td><td>Aircraft is slowed to just above stall speed. Positive control is mostly maintained with pitch, power, yaw, and roll control. Instructor may take over in event of a stall or loss of positive control.</td><td>Aircraft is slowed to just above stall speed. Positive control is maintained with pitch, power, yaw, and roll control. Any stalls are immediately recovered from.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Fly a left hand traffic pattern with a go around, do not land.</th><td>Little or no legs are completed without the instructor taking over. Most of the flying is uncoordinated and unstable.</td><td>Most of a left hand traffic pattern consisting of downwind, base, final, upwind, and cross wind is performed without error or instructor control. Instructor may have to execute a go around, or some of the pattern may be unstable.</td><td>A complete left hand traffic pattern consisting of downwind, base, final, upwind, and cross wind is performed without error or instructor control. A go around and properly performed by student.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<h3>Skills introduced and demonstrated by the instructor</h3><p class="small">May be performed in front of a large group one time.</p><ul class="skills-checklist">
<li>Aileron roll</li>
<li>Loop</li>
<li>Stall/recovery</li>
<li>Left-hand traffic pattern</li>
<li>Power off landing</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="flight-3">3rd Flight Airplane</h2>
<p><strong>Goal:</strong> Demonstrate in flight control of airplane for one or more circuits of the field with no instructor control. Attempt first hand-launch take-off if instructor approves. Attempt first landing if instructor approves.</p>
<div class="textbook-table activity-table" role="region" aria-label="3rd Flight Airplane rubric" tabindex="0"><table><caption>3rd Flight Airplane rubric</caption><thead><tr><th scope="col">Demonstrated skill</th><th scope="col">Beginner</th><th scope="col">Intermediate</th><th scope="col">Mastery</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th scope="row">Full circuit of the flying field without instructor control.</th><td>Student can only fly some of the circuit. Student loses positive control, has trouble holding altitude, or becomes disoriented. Instructor has to take control often.</td><td>Student can fly most of the circuit of the field (all legs of the traffic pattern except altitude is maintained the entire time). Some instructor input is required. Most of the circuit is stable.</td><td>Student can fly the full circuit of the field (all legs of the traffic pattern except altitude is maintained the entire time). No instructor input is required. Entire circuit is stable.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Hand launch take off. (Someone other than the student will throw the airplane, student is ready at the controls).</th><td>Student crashes or instructor has to take over because very little positive control is demonstrated. Student is unable to recover aircraft in flight.</td><td>Instructor has to take over, but aircraft does not crash and/or Student maintains control but exceeds pitch and roll limits before regaining control.</td><td>Student applies full power, climbs to altitude determined by the instructor, and aircraft does not exceed 30 degrees of pitch, or 30 degrees of bank.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Landing attempt, instructor may take control.</th><td>Student does not demonstrate the control required for a landing attempt. Instructor has to take control repeatedly. Most of the flight/approach is unstable.</td><td>Student can fly most legs of the traffic pattern but instructor takes over briefly to stabilize aircraft. Final approach is not stabilized. Student does not need to land.</td><td>Student can fly all legs of the traffic pattern and get the aircraft to a stabilized final approach. Student does not need to land.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Perform slow flight while maintaining positive control of aircraft.</th><td>Student attempts slow flight but is unable to maintain positive control for more than a few seconds before control is taken. Student often stalls the aircraft or otherwise loses control.</td><td>Aircraft is slowed to just above stall speed. Positive control is mostly maintained with pitch, power, yaw, and roll control. Instructor may take over in event of a stall or loss of positive control.</td><td>Aircraft is slowed to just above stall speed. Positive control is maintained with pitch, power, yaw, and roll control. Any stalls are immediately recovered from.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Fly a left hand traffic pattern.</th><td>Little or no legs are completed without the instructor taking over. Most of the flying is uncoordinated and unstable.</td><td>Most of a left hand traffic pattern consisting of downwind, base, final, upwind, and cross wind is performed without error or instructor control. Instructor may have to execute a go around, or some of the pattern may be unstable.</td><td>All of a left hand traffic pattern consisting of downwind, base, final, upwind, and cross wind is performed without error or instructor control.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<h2 id="flight-4">4th Flight Airplane</h2>
<p><strong>Goal:</strong> Demonstrate all pre-solo skills and refine/practice weak skills. Student should be at or approaching the skills required for a solo flight.</p>
<div class="textbook-table activity-table" role="region" aria-label="4th Flight Airplane rubric" tabindex="0"><table><caption>4th Flight Airplane rubric</caption><thead><tr><th scope="col">Demonstrated skill</th><th scope="col">Beginner</th><th scope="col">Intermediate</th><th scope="col">Mastery</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th scope="row">Hand launch the airplane (student throws the airplane).</th><td>Student crashes or instructor has to take over because very little positive control is demonstrated. Student is unable to recover aircraft in flight.</td><td>Instructor has to take over, but aircraft does not crash and/or Student maintains control but exceeds pitch and roll limits before regaining control.</td><td>Student applies full power, climbs to altitude determined by the instructor, and aircraft does not exceed 30 degrees of pitch, or 30 degrees of bank.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Climb up to and maintain altitude of 50-100’ as determined by instructor.</th><td>Aircraft fails to reach altitude, overshoots altitude, or is unable to maintain altitude to +/- 50 feet or better.</td><td>Aircraft climbs up to but might overshoot determined altitude. Student maintains altitude +/- 50 feet as determined by instructor.</td><td>Aircraft climbs up to but does not overshoot determined altitude. Student maintains altitude +/- 20 feet as determined by instructor.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Make 3 full circuits of the flying field maintaining positive control of the aircraft at all times.</th><td>Student can only fly some of the circuit. Entire circuit is stable.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Perform slow flight while maintaining positive control of aircraft at altitude determined by instructor.</th><td>Student attempts slow flight but is unable to maintain positive control for more than a few seconds before control is taken. Student often stalls the aircraft or otherwise loses control.</td><td>Aircraft is slowed to just above stall speed. Positive control is mostly maintained with pitch, power, yaw, and roll control. Instructor may take over in event of a stall or loss of positive control.</td><td>Aircraft is slowed to just above stall speed. Positive control is maintained with pitch, power, yaw, and roll control. Any stalls are immediately recovered from.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Fly a left hand traffic pattern.</th><td>Little or no legs are completed without the instructor taking over. Most of the flying is uncoordinated and unstable.</td><td>Most of a left hand traffic pattern consisting of downwind, base, final, upwind, and cross wind is performed without error or instructor control. Instructor may have to execute a go around, or some of the pattern may be unstable.</td><td>All of a left hand traffic pattern consisting of downwind, base, final, upwind, and cross wind is performed without error or instructor control.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Land inside designated touchdown zone with no damage to the aircraft.</th><td>Aircraft crashes or is damaged, lands outside the designated landing zone, or the instructor had to take over.</td><td>Aircraft is landed without damage in the designated touchdown zone with some instructor input.</td><td>Aircraft is landed without damage in the designated touchdown zone without and instructor input.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<h2 id="flight-5">5th Flight Airplane</h2>
