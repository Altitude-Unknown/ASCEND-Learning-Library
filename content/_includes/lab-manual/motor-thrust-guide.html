This guide is intended to help you set up and navigate the motors, esc, batteries and ohms law lab. Ask your instructor for the motor mount’s 3D print files. Print parts from PETG. Make sure test fixtures are securely clamped. Wear safety glasses and use caution around the spinning propellers.

Materials: Safety glasses, brushless motor, ESC, receiver, battery, transmitter, fish scale, clamp current meter, 3D printed motor test fixture, clamp(s).

Procedure:

Step 1: Begin by mounting a brushless motor to the 3D printed motor mount secured with 4 motor mounting screws. Note that the hole pattern may not be symmetrical. Line up all of the screws and finger tighten first before you tighten them all. Make sure the screw lengths are not so long that they hit the windings inside the motor. Doing so can destroy the motor.

Step 2: Slide the motor mount into the 3D printed motor mount base and clamp the base to a solid workbench as shown. Make sure that the propeller can spin freely and won’t hit people, the table, the clamp, or any loose wires. If you feel that one clamp is not enough, clamp both sides of the motor mount. Attaching the motor mount to a wooden board as shown below allows for easier clamping.

Step 3: Connect your motor to your ESC.

Step 4: Connect your ESC’s BEC (Battery Elimination Circuit) to your receiver.

Step 5: Bind your receiver to your transmitter if you have not already done so.

Step 6: Connect your fish scale hook to the hole at the rear of your 3D printed motor mount (the part that slides).

Step 7: Connect your DC clamp-on amp meter as shown. Make sure to only clamp around the red power wire coming off of your battery. Do NOT clamp both the power and the ground. Make sure the current meter is set to measure DC amps NOT AC amps. Also, if there is more than one current setting, select the range you expect to measure. For example if there is a 50 amp range and a 500 amp range, select the 50 amp range as it is likely closer to what you would measure on a small RC motor. Typical current measurements will be between 10-40 amps for small motors such as those pictured here. Larger motors could go over 100 amps however.

Step 8: Securely mount a propeller to your motor. Make sure the writing on the propeller is facing forward. Secure the prop nut. Use caution as the motor could start at any point and the spinning propeller can cause serious injury! Make sure motor rotation direction is correct or injury could occur!

Before applying full power make sure motor rotation is correct. A motor spinning in the wrong direction will push the motor out of the test fixture and can cause injury to you or others! Confirm with your instructor that you have the motor rotation correct for the type of propellor you have installed!

Step 9: SLOWLY power up your motor (advance the throttle on your transmitter) to full power while holding onto the fish scale. Use caution near the spinning propeller! Make sure to get the motor mount slide into a neutral middle position so that the motor thrust is trying to pull the fish scale forward while you resist the pull. You want a good steady pull for an accurate measurement. Measure the force in kg that your motor and propeller are producing. Record the data in your lab sheet.

Step 10: With the throttle still at full power, read the current on the current meter and record the data in your lab sheet.

Step 11: Replace your 3 cell battery with a 4 cell battery and repeat steps 8 and 9.

Step 12: Replace your propellor with a larger, smaller, or one with more blades, then repeat steps 8-10.
